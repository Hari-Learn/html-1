Multimodal Audio Processing & Speech Workflows

Understanding audio pipelines, transcription and voice-agent architecture.

1. What is Audio Processing?

Audio processing means converting spoken sound into information that an application can understand and use.

ELI10 analogy

Imagine a person calls customer support and says, "Where is my order?" A voice system must first understand what the person said before deciding what action to take.

2. The Audio Processing Pipeline

Customer speaks
↓
Audio capture
↓
Speech recognition / transcription
↓
Language understanding
↓
Claude reasoning and tool selection
↓
Response to customer

This is a general voice-agent architecture. The exact implementation depends on which audio and speech services are integrated.

3. Speech-to-Text vs Text-to-Speech

Speech-to-Text (STT)

Converts spoken audio into written text.

Audio → Text

Example: "Cancel my order" becomes a text transcript.

Text-to-Speech (TTS)

Converts written text into spoken audio.

Text → Audio

Example: "Your order is shipped" becomes spoken output.

4. Why Transcription Quality Matters

A transcription error can become a reasoning error.

Customer actually says:

"I did not receive the package."

Incorrect transcript:

"I received the package."

Potential consequence:

The agent may make the wrong delivery-status decision.

Exam trap: Better reasoning cannot reliably repair incorrect input data.

5. Audio Challenges in Production

  • Background noise: Traffic, music and other voices.
  • Accents: Different pronunciations and speech patterns.
  • Multiple speakers: Two people speaking simultaneously.
  • Ambiguous words: Similar-sounding names or numbers.
  • Latency: Long processing time before a response.
  • Privacy: Audio may contain sensitive personal information.

6. Speaker Diarization

Speaker diarization identifies which speaker said which part of a recording.

Speaker 1: I need to cancel my order.

Speaker 2: May I know your order number?

Speaker 1: It is ORD123.

This is useful in call-centre analytics, interviews and meeting summaries.

Diarization identifies speaker turns; it does not necessarily identify the real-world person.

7. Voice Agent Reliability

For sensitive actions, do not act on an uncertain transcript alone.

  • Ask the customer to repeat unclear details.
  • Confirm important numbers and names.
  • Require explicit confirmation for high-impact actions.
  • Check authorization before executing tools.
  • Provide a human handoff when confidence is insufficient.

8. CCAO-F Scenario Challenge

A banking voice assistant receives a request to transfer ₹50,000. Due to background noise, the transcript may contain either ₹15,000 or ₹50,000. What is the safest response?

A. Choose the more common amount and execute the transfer.

B. Ask the customer to confirm the exact amount, then validate authorization and transaction rules.

C. Increase Claude's temperature.

D. Ignore the transcript and use the customer's previous transaction amount.

Reveal answer

Correct answer: B

The amount is ambiguous and the action has financial consequences. Confirm the critical input before execution.

9. Notebook Revision

  • STT = Speech to Text.
  • TTS = Text to Speech.
  • Diarization = identifying speaker turns.
  • Transcription errors can cause reasoning errors.
  • Confirm uncertain details before sensitive actions.
  • Protect audio data and sensitive transcripts.
